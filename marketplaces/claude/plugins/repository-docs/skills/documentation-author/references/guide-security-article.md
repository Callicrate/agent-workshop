# Security Article Workflow

Use this guide for customer-facing security articles, public research summaries, vendor-sensitive guidance, and article drafts built from source notes.

## Workflow

1. Inventory source material before drafting: original public URLs, vendor posts, advisories, internal notes, quoted text, and customer-impact observations.
2. Draft an outline before prose. Separate threat summary, evidence, customer impact, recommendations, and caveats.
3. Convert internal notes into customer-facing guidance. Keep recommendations concrete and defensive.
4. Use partner-neutral wording. Do not assign blame or imply vendor negligence unless the source and user intent support that framing.
5. Cite original public URLs when possible. Use secondary summaries only when the original source is unavailable or not the right citation target.
6. Format direct quotes separately from paraphrase, quote only the needed excerpt, and keep the surrounding claim narrower than the source supports.
7. Run a claim audit before finalizing: novelty, timeline, affected products, attacker behavior, defensive advice, and source support.
8. Keep research status precise. Do not imply measurements, detections, exploitation results, or customer impact exists before the source material or user-provided evidence supports it.

## Checks

- Claims of novelty do not overstate what sources already reported.
- Recommendations are actionable for customers, not only descriptive.
- Titles and headings do not create unnecessary partner conflict.
- Direct quotes are attributed and visually distinct from the surrounding prose.
- Source links point to the source that actually supports the sentence nearby.
- Internal-only context is rewritten or omitted when the output is public-facing.
- Draft abstracts and summaries separate observed facts from planned analysis, proposed methodology, and open questions.
