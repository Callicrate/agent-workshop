# Domain Playbooks

Use these quick expansions when the artifact falls into a common genre.

## White paper or research-style document

Prioritize:

- novelty claims
- literature or benchmark selection
- whether caveats are buried
- whether the evaluation setup matches the deployment claim
- whether conclusions outrun the evidence
- whether planned work, hypotheses, or open questions are presented as completed findings

## Project proposal or business case

Prioritize:

- ROI assumptions
- staffing and dependency assumptions
- implementation realism
- stakeholder incentives
- omitted costs, integration effort, and change-management burden

## Strategy memo or market thesis

Prioritize:

- TAM, SAM, and adoption assumptions
- competitor framing and substitute products
- switching costs and distribution constraints
- base rates for similar initiatives
- downside cases and sensitivity analysis

## Technical design or architecture note

Prioritize:

- correctness
- complexity and scaling claims
- failure modes
- security and privacy boundaries
- retries, idempotency, consistency, and observability
- operational burden and rollback

## Policy memo or governance document

Prioritize:

- causal inference quality
- stakeholder distribution effects
- legal and compliance assumptions
- unintended consequences
- omitted alternatives and implementation constraints

## Partner-sensitive security article or external report

Prioritize:

- whether each threat, vendor, product, and timeline claim is supported by the cited source
- whether original public URLs are used instead of secondary summaries when possible
- whether direct quotes are exact, attributed, visually distinct, and not asked to support broader claims than they contain
- whether novelty claims overstate what prior public sources already reported
- whether claims about observed exploitation, detections, measurements, or customer impact are supported rather than inferred from planned analysis
- whether customer recommendations are concrete, defensive, and proportional to the evidence
- whether the title, headings, and framing create unnecessary partner blame or vendor conflict
- whether internal notes, private context, or unsupported assumptions leaked into public-facing prose
