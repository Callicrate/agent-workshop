# Intake automation proposal

## Recommendation
Buy a one-year $180,000 license now. The headline case says: "At 10,000 tickets per month and six minutes saved per ticket, we recover 1,000 hours monthly, worth $50,000 at $50 per hour. Annual net savings are $420,000. The system pays for itself."

## Evidence
A vendor-sponsored two-week pilot on 200 preselected simple tickets measured six minutes less handling time per ticket. Complex tickets were excluded. Two trade articles repeat the vendor's pilot results; neither collected independent data. No accuracy, rework, or end-to-end queue-time results are supplied. We have not audited the raw pilot data.

## Internal operations note
Only 30% of monthly tickets are in the simple category. Each automated simple ticket still needs two minutes of human review, not included in the six-minute claim. Finance says saved hours would be reassigned to backlog work, not removed from payroll. Integration is estimated at $60,000 in year one; no maintenance estimate exists. The existing rules engine has not been compared on the same tickets.

## Appendix: proposed controls
During rollout, the intake lead would review every automated ticket before any external action. The vendor can be disabled and the current manual queue retained. This is a proposed control, not a tested deployment. A four-week capped evaluation is available for $5,000; it creates no annual purchase obligation.
